\begin{abstract}
We follow the existing positive-cosmological-constant quantization of
Kim--Scarinci to identify a concrete local Hilbert-space benchmark for
the missing observer gluing problem. We give an explicit four-puncture
Heisenberg representation, distinguish phase pairing from a modulus
square, and implement the stated pentagon in two discretizations.
Scalar controls agree, but our non-flat operator reproduction remains
unresolved. This is a diagnostic record, not a refutation of the
continuum theorem or a construction of a finite-$G$ observer dual.
\end{abstract}
\section{What is known, and what is being tested}
CLP's fixed-topology continuation does not itself supply a general
de Sitter gluing theory; its discussion points to both quantization
and the non-diagonal physical Gram problem \cite{clp,cem}.
The KS construction supplies explicit unitary local operators on
punctured-surface Hilbert spaces \cite{ks,kim}. Its full flip also
contains a linear, or Shale--Weil, intertwiner. Here we test the
separately stated two-variable pentagon, not a complete flip network.

Use dimensionless coordinates, $\Lambda=+1$, and $\hbar>0$.
The proposed comparison $b^2=i/\kappa=i\hbar$ matches deformation
parameters; it is not an observer mass dictionary. The source functions are
\begin{align}
 \psi_q(w)&=\prod_{n=0}^\infty(1+q^{2n+1}w)^{-1},\\
 \Phi^{i\hbar}(z)&=
 \frac{\psi_{e^{-\pi\hbar}}(e^z)}
      {\psi_{e^{-\pi/\hbar}}(e^{z/(i\hbar)})},\qquad z=x+i\hbar y,\\
 \F(x,y)&=\Phi^{i\hbar}(z)\Phi^{-i\hbar}(\bar z)
       =\frac{\Phi^{i\hbar}(z)}{\overline{\Phi^{i\hbar}(z)}}.
 \label{phase}
\end{align}
Consequently $|\F|=1$ almost everywhere. Isolated exceptional points can
be assigned arbitrary unit values for the Lebesgue multiplication
operator. This phase ratio is distinct from $|\Phi|^2$.
Neither a scalar modulus square nor scalar positivity identifies an
inner product for general mixed four-point pairings.

\section{An explicit four-puncture representation}
Order the edges as $12,13,14,23,24,34$, and orient faces as
$(132),(124),(143),(234)$. Let $V_{ep}=1$ when $p$ is an endpoint
of $e$, and zero otherwise. The oriented exchange matrix is
\begin{equation}
 E=UJU^T,\qquad
 U=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\-1&-1\\-1&-1\\0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},
 \quad J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Here $\operatorname{rank}E=2$, $\operatorname{rank}V=4$,
$EV=0$ and $V^TU=0$. On
$\HH=L^2(\mathbb R^2,dt_1dt_2)$ put
\begin{equation}
 p_a=-\pi i\partial_{t_a},\qquad
 \mathbf x=U\mathbf p,\qquad \mathbf y=UJ\mathbf t.
 \label{rep}
\end{equation}
On Schwartz functions, and in the Weyl sense after closure,
\begin{equation}
 [x_i,x_j]=[y_i,y_j]=0,\quad
 [x_i,y_j]=\pi iE_{ij},\quad
 V^T\mathbf x=V^T\mathbf y=0.
\end{equation}
For example $(x_1,y_1)=(p_1,t_2)$ and
$(x_2,y_2)=(p_2,-t_1)$. Each same-edge pair strongly commutes.
The four operators on these two edges recover two canonical pairs,
so this representation is irreducible. This is an explicit instance
of the known constraint reduction, not a new quantization theorem.

There is also a useful purely algebraic extension. Define
\begin{equation}
 R=V(V^TV)^{-1},\qquad
 \mathbf x_\xi=U\mathbf p+R\xi,\qquad
 \mathbf y_\eta=UJ\mathbf t+R\eta,\qquad \xi,\eta\in\mathbb R^4.
\end{equation}
Then $V^T\mathbf x_\xi=\xi$ and $V^T\mathbf y_\eta=\eta$,
with the same commutators, self-adjointness and local unitarity.
This does \emph{not} establish that these central characters are
conical masses, nor construct all intertwiners between triangulations.

\paragraph{Relation to the CLP special function.}
With $b^2=i\hbar$, $Q=b+b^{-1}$ and $w=2\pi ib\zeta$,
CLP (3.48) becomes
\begin{equation}
 S_b(Q/2+\zeta)=
 \Phi^{i\hbar}(w)
 \exp\left[-\frac{\pi i}{2}\zeta^2+
                  \frac{i\pi}{24}(Q^2-2)\right].
\end{equation}
Thus the same compact products occur, but the Gaussian prefactor
and pairing operation must be retained. Equality of special-function
ingredients does not identify their states or gluing measures.
CEM's $h+\tilde h^*=1$ applies to weights in one wavefunction;
it should not be confused with CLP's paired-factor $h_++h_-=1$.

\section{A mixed Gram test and the pentagon}
For calculation it is convenient to use the unitarily equivalent
realization
\begin{equation}
 (x_1,y_1)=(x,y),\qquad (x_2,y_2)=(p_y,p_x),
 \qquad p_x=-\pi i\partial_x,\quad p_y=-\pi i\partial_y .
\end{equation}
It has $[x_1,y_2]=[y_1,x_2]=\pi i$ and all other required
commutators zero. Let
\begin{equation}
 A=\F(x,y),\quad B=\F(p_y,p_x),\quad
 M=e^{ixy/\pi},\quad C=M^{-1}BM.
\end{equation}
The exact differential identities
$M^{-1}p_yM=p_y+x$ and $M^{-1}p_xM=p_x+y$ give
\begin{equation}
 C=\F(x+p_y,y+p_x).
\end{equation}
The source pentagon (KS v2, Proposition 6.15; Kim v2, (3.8)) is
\begin{equation}
 AB=BCA=BM^{-1}BMA. \label{pent}
\end{equation}
Our numerical residual is the norm of the difference of these two
words acting on the normalized state
\begin{equation}
 \psi(x,y)=\pi^{-1}\exp[-(x^2+y^2)/(2\pi)] .
 \label{state}
\end{equation}
No observer time or physical OTOC is assigned to these operators.

The different words $AB$ and $BA$ are used for a separate Gram check.
For $u=AB\psi$, $v=BA\psi$ and $z=\langle u,v\rangle$,
\begin{equation}
 G=\begin{pmatrix}1&z\\\bar z&1\end{pmatrix}\succeq0,\qquad
 D=\|u-v\|^2=2-2\operatorname{Re}z .
\end{equation}
This follows from the stated Hilbert space and unitarity; it does not
imply (\ref{pent}). In particular, a discretization made of unitary
matrices can pass every norm/Gram check while failing its continuum
operator target.

\paragraph{Two implementations.}
The periodic FFT uses nodes $x_j=-L+2Lj/N$ and a two-dimensional
unitary Fourier transform. Its momentum grid is
\[
p_k=2\pi^2\operatorname{fftfreq}(N,2L/N).
\]
The Hermite DVR uses eigenvectors $Q_{nj}$
of the Jacobi matrix with off-diagonal entries $\sqrt{n/2}$.
Its physical nodes are $\sqrt{\pi}$ times the Hermite nodes and
its Fourier matrix is
\begin{equation}
 T=Q^T\operatorname{diag}((-i)^n)Q .
\end{equation}
Both implement $B=T^\dagger\F(p_y,p_x)T$ and the same chirp
factorization, with the same continuum Gaussian target.
Finite matrices do not satisfy exact canonical Weyl relations.

\section{Results: scalar agreement, operator residual}
There are 21 matched-protocol runs: three FFT grids, four Hermite
orders, and three multipliers at each setting. The flat control uses
$F_0(x,y)=\exp[-iy\log(1+e^x)/\pi]$; it is the independently defined
$\Lambda=0$ formula, not a claimed numerical $\hbar\to0$ limit.

Entries are $\|(AB-BM^{-1}BMA)\psi\|$. The non-flat reproduction
is unresolved; we do not mark it as passed.
At Hermite $N=512,\hbar=1$ the order-exchange overlap is approximately
$.94417241-.00563230i$, giving $D=.11165518$.
These are results of the discrete benchmark, not finite-$G$
observer correlations.

Thirty-six scalar/algebra checks passed. They include independent
55-digit compact products, the inversion identity
\begin{equation}
 \F(x,y)\F(-x,-y)=e^{-ixy/\pi},
\end{equation}
three slanted Barnes-integral controls, exact puncture matrices,
and the chirp conjugation. The Barnes line is
$p=e^{-i\pi/4}(t+0.2i)$ with $-30<t<30$ at $\hbar=1$.
This quadrature has empirical, not interval-certified, errors.

For a compact-product logarithm, truncate after $n=N-1$ and set
$r_0=e^{a-(2N+1)r}<1$. Its exact-arithmetic logarithmic tail obeys
\begin{equation}
 \left|\sum_{n=N}^\infty\log(1+e^{a-(2n+1)r+ib})\right|
 \leq\frac{r_0}{(1-e^{-2r})(1-r_0)}.
\end{equation}
Twice the sum of the two tails bounds the scalar phase error.
It does not bound floating-point errors, aliasing, or Hermite
functional-calculus errors.

The 161-check evidence replay verifies hashes, run counts and Gram
arithmetic while explicitly retaining the unresolved status.
Earlier prototypes used different states/representations and are
preserved separately. Neither their norm preservation nor the
agreement between two discretizations is a continuum counterexample.

\section{A controlled next test}
The next calculation should replace nodewise DVR functional calculus
by actual Hermite Galerkin matrix elements. This matters because a DVR
phase multiplier is exactly unitary at every finite order, concealing
the leakage of a projected continuum state.

Here is an elementary error ledger for that task. Let $P$ be a finite
orthogonal Hermite projector, $U_j$ the actual bounded unitary
multiplication or Fourier-conjugated multiplication operators, and
$T_j=PU_jP$. For $\psi\in P\HH$, define
$v_0=\psi$, $v_j=T_jv_{j-1}$. Then
\begin{align}
 \ell_j^2&=\|(1-P)U_jv_{j-1}\|^2
          =\|v_{j-1}\|^2-\|v_j\|^2,\\
 \|U_m\cdots U_1\psi-v_m\|&\leq\sum_{j=1}^m\ell_j .
 \label{leak}
\end{align}
The proof is a one-step telescoping triangle inequality and
orthogonality of $P$. It requires true compressed operators;
substituting a unitary DVR matrix makes the leakage estimate invalid.

If computed matrices $\widetilde T_j$ satisfy
$\|\widetilde T_j-T_j\|\leq\epsilon_j$, the difference of their
products is bounded by
\begin{equation}
 \|\widetilde T_m\cdots\widetilde T_1-T_m\cdots T_1\|
 \leq\sum_{j=1}^m\epsilon_j\prod_{k<j}(1+\epsilon_k),
\end{equation}
since $\|T_j\|\leq1$. A finite observed residual $r$ can lower-bound
the continuum residual only after both word errors are controlled:
\begin{equation}
 \|(AB-BM^{-1}BMA)\psi\|\geq r-\delta_{\rm left}-\delta_{\rm right}.
\end{equation}
This is an actionable certification target, not a completed estimate.
Scalar zero/pole neighborhoods must be excised with norm bounds or
integrated with appropriate local coordinates. The Gaussian tails,
quadrature errors and Hermite leakage must all be recorded.

\paragraph{Open physical interfaces.}
Even a successful continuum reproduction would leave the mass and holonomy
map, boundary states, full triangulation intertwiners, and the physical
observer insertions and Gram gluing unresolved. Conversely, the current
numerical difficulty is not evidence that de Sitter holography is
impossible. We keep the original scientific objective open.
