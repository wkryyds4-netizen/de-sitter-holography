\begin{abstract}
We fix principal-series conventions, shadow equivalence and the spectral
measure needed for a proposed $G=0$ recoil dictionary. A spectral rectangle
removes arbitrary local channel normalizations. For one explicit
topology-preserving scalar identification, its value for the known global
conformal $6j$ differs from both the recoil function $H$ and the paired
graph $Z_{\rm rec}$. High precision and an independent Euler representation
support the mismatch; no interval certificate is claimed. A separate
positivity argument rules out a continuous nonnegative kernel as a
complete scalar-only unitary transform. Neither result excludes nonlocal
dictionaries or constructs finite-gravity de Sitter dynamics.
\end{abstract}

\section{Principal series and the meaning of a barred weight}
The reference harmonic analysis is on a Euclidean two-dimensional
boundary. We set the de Sitter radius to one when comparing labels;
no physical gravitational inner product is inferred from this choice.
For bosonic single-valued representations,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=1+i\nu,\quad J\in\mathbb Z,\qquad
 h=\frac{1+J+i\nu}{2},\quad \bar h=\frac{1-J+i\nu}{2}.
 \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
Here $\bar h$ is an antiholomorphic label, \emph{not} complex
conjugation. Unitarity obeys $h^*+\bar h=1$; a scalar has
$h=\bar h=(1+i\nu)/2$. The single-representation space has inner
product $\int_{\C}\dd^2z\,f(z)^*g(z)$ \cite{kks}.
This is not yet the Hilbert space of the paired Virasoro theory.

Use equation (5) of Derkachov--Spiridonov (DS) \cite{ds} to fix
their integer convention:
\begin{equation}
 a=m/2+i\sigma,\quad\bar a=-m/2+i\sigma,\quad
 h=(1-a)/2,\quad\bar h=(1-\bar a)/2.
\end{equation}
Thus $J=-m/2$ and $\nu=-\sigma$. We restrict to even $m$.
Distinct scalar external legs still permit every integer internal
spin. Identical-field symmetry restrictions require a separate
exchange-parity analysis.

\section{Reflection, measure and the two-factor obstruction}
DS equations (22), (28)--(30) contain a direct
$\delta_R(a-a')$ contribution and a reflected $\delta_R(a+a')$
contribution multiplied by a shadow kernel. In their CG convention,
\begin{equation}
 \rho_{\rm DS}(a)=-\frac{a\bar a}{4\pi^4}
 =\frac{m^2/4+\sigma^2}{4\pi^4},\qquad
 \int\frac{\rho_{\rm DS}(a)}2D_Ra
 =\frac12\sum_{m\in2\mathbb Z}\int_{\R}\dd\sigma\,\rho_{\rm DS}(a).
\end{equation}
The factor $1/2$ accounts for reflected equivalent representations.
Choosing a fundamental domain also changes basis bookkeeping;
one cannot simply discard the reflection term. The Racah transform
in DS (32) uses this measure. Their Barnes formula (45) contains
an integer sum and an integral. Scalar external legs alone do not
remove the sum.

In the distinct KKS normalization, equations (73)--(74) give
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal N(\Delta,0)=
 \frac{\pi^2\Gamma(1-\Delta)\Gamma(\Delta-1)}
 {\Gamma(\Delta)\Gamma(2-\Delta)}
 =\frac{\pi^2}{\nu^2},\qquad
 \mu_{\rm KKS}=\frac{1}{4\mathcal N\,\operatorname{vol}SO(2)}.
\end{equation}
Their partial-wave norm also includes two three-point pairings,
equation (39). Raw DS density, KKS measure and a four-point inverse
norm are not interchangeable.

For $\widehat f(p)=\int\dd^2x\,e^{-ip\cdot x}f(x)$, the
distributionally continued scalar shadow kernel
$|x-y|^{-2(2-\Delta)}$ has multiplier
\begin{equation}
 \widehat S_\Delta(p)=
 \pi\,2^{2i\nu}\frac{\Gamma(i\nu)}{\Gamma(1-i\nu)}|p|^{-2i\nu}.
\end{equation}
Its modulus is $\pi/\nu$ for $\nu>0$. Multiplication by $\nu/\pi$
defines a unitary Fourier multiplier on $L^2(\C)$; $p=0$
has measure zero. The endpoint $\nu=0$ is excluded.

The algebraic relation
$z(M)=M^2e^{-2\pi M}=4\pi^4e^{-2\pi M}\rho_{\rm DS}(iM)$
does not derive the physical observer measure. The exponential
weight is additional state or preparation information.

The common-angle, fixed-mass limit of the two CLP factors
\cite{clp}, checked in our preceding note, is
\begin{equation}
 h_+=(1-i\mu)/2,\qquad h_-=(1+i\mu)/2.
\end{equation}
Identifying these directly with $(h,\bar h)$ gives
$J=-i\mu$, incompatible with \eqref{eq:weights} for generic real
$\mu$. Formally replacing a factor by a dual label is not a
constructed physical shadow transform. We instead test a separately
defined global scalar dictionary below.

\section{The global matrix element and the candidate map}
The LPRS conformal $6j$ \cite{lprs} is defined by the bilinear
overlap of a shadow partial wave and a partial wave, their (3.5).
It is not itself a unit-normalized matrix. Changing channel norms
only produces row and column factors.

All weights below are scalar: $h_i=\bar h_i$, $h=\bar h$ and
$h'=\bar h'$. Set $h_{ij}=h_i-h_j$, $p=h_2+h_3$. LPRS
(3.17), (3.35)--(3.37) give
\begin{align}
 K_q^{ab}
 &=\pi\frac{\Gamma(2q-1)}{\Gamma(2-2q)}
 \frac{\Gamma(1-q-h_{ab})\Gamma(1-q+h_{ab})}
 {\Gamma(q-h_{ab})\Gamma(q+h_{ab})},\\
 \Omega_{x,y}
 &=\int_0^1\dd u\,u^{x+p+h_{13}-2}(1-u)^{y-p}\nonumber\\
 &\quad{}\times{}_2F_1(x+h_{12},x-h_{34};2x;u)
 {}_2F_1(y-h_{32},y+h_{14};2y;1-u),\label{eq:euler}\\
 B(h,q)
 &=\frac{\Gamma(h+h_{12})\Gamma(h-h_{12})
 \Gamma(1-h+h_{43})\Gamma(1-h+h_{34})}
 {4\Gamma(2h)\Gamma(2-2h)}\nonumber\\
 &\quad{}\times\sin\pi(q-h_1-h_4)\sin\pi(q-h_2-h_3)
 \Omega_{h,q}\Omega_{1-h,q},\\
 \J(h,h')&=K_{1-h'}^{14}B(h,h')+K_{h'}^{32}B(h,1-h').
 \label{eq:J}
\end{align}
The implementation retains both shadow terms in \eqref{eq:J}
and both \({}_4F_3\) terms in each LPRS (3.38) expression for
$\Omega$. Equation \eqref{eq:euler} is an independent check.
For our samples its endpoint real powers are $-1/2$, with at
most logarithmic factors, so the integral converges.

The frozen recoil graph has vertices $V=\{123,156,246,345\}$.
Its opposite internal edges are $\mu_3=s$ and $\mu_6=t$.
The explicit assignment is

\section{A normalization-independent numerical test}
For four nonzero matrix elements, define
\begin{equation}
 \CR[K]=\frac{K(s_1,t_1)K(s_2,t_2)}
 {K(s_1,t_2)K(s_2,t_1)}.
\end{equation}
Substitution proves $\CR[ABK]=\CR[K]$. Use
\begin{equation}
 \begin{gathered}
 (\mu_1,\mu_2,\mu_4,\mu_5)=(.4,.7,.93,.6),\\
 (s_1,s_2)=(.65,.85),\qquad(t_1,t_2)=(.57,.81).
 \end{gathered}
\end{equation}
All vertices satisfy strict triangle inequalities and the left
and right recoil residue families are nondegenerate.
At 60 decimal digits,
\begin{align}
 \CR[\J]&=0.97254633802865920965+O(10^{-60})i,\\
 \CR[H]&=1.00800263623569531689-0.03406416860860421339i,\\
 \CR[Z_{\rm rec}]&=1.01722968224110691421.
\end{align}
The relative discrepancies $|\CR[K]/\CR[\J]-1|$ are
$0.05055620141488165$ and $0.04594469431967669$.
Equation \eqref{eq:Z} implies $\CR[Z_{\rm rec}]=|\CR[H]|^2$:
the six leg factors cancel.

These differences survive local leg, vertex and channel
renormalizations under the stated map. They are
\emph{finite-point numerical counterexample evidence}, not an
interval-certified inequality. They do not exclude every physical
dictionary or the CLP fixed-topology proposal.

There are 44 passed diagnostics: eight left/right recoil checks,
12 pointwise precision comparisons, three rectangle refinements,

a Gamma-pole exception in the separated formula. The initial driver
and failure record were saved. We moved to the generic rectangle
without changing frozen recoil code or weakening tolerances.
This formula-domain failure is not a demonstrated singularity of $H$.

\section{An exact obstruction and the next physical task}
\begin{proposition}
Let two continuous label spaces carry locally finite positive Borel
measures with full support on open intervals. A continuous,
everywhere nonnegative kernel cannot define a complete unitary
integral transform between their $L^2$ spaces.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A nonzero kernel is strictly positive on some open rectangle.
Choose two nonnegative, nonzero input test functions with disjoint
compact supports within its input interval. Their inner product
vanishes, while both outputs are strictly positive on the output
interval. Nonnegativity elsewhere prevents cancellation, so their
output inner product is positive. If either output fails to lie
in $L^2$, unitarity already fails. The zero kernel is not unitary.
\end{proof}
Thus the nonnegative real-mass $Z_{\rm rec}$ cannot be a
\emph{complete scalar-only} unitary recoupling kernel repaired
solely by positive weights. Separable basis phases can be removed
by unitary rephasings. This does not apply to complex $H$, a scalar
subblock of a spin-summed unitary transform, a Euclidean amplitude,
a probability kernel, or a physical correlator. Missing spin
channels matter for orthogonality.

The next task should derive a physical insertion: start with
$G=0$ dS$_3$ Bunch--Davies positive-frequency modes, Klein--Gordon
normalization and observer representations. Define the local-field
matrix element and its invariant or group-averaged three-point
integral; match its full three-label coefficient and reflection
factors to the DS CG kernel. Covariance alone leaves coupling
coefficients and does not fix dynamics. This comparison can test
whether the undetermined two-point normalization $n$ is fixed.

A restriction to spin zero must specify actual projectors and
the resulting incompleteness. Retain the independent free-probe,
large-observer-mass benchmark
\begin{equation}
 g_\nu(0)g_{\nu'}(0)=
 \frac{\nu\nu'}{16\pi^2\sinh\pi\nu\,\sinh\pi\nu'}.
\end{equation}
This is a disconnected antipodal product, not a zero-energy graph
value. Physical finite-$\kappa$ sewing, finite-$G$ dynamics and
complete de Sitter holography remain open. This note narrows a
candidate dictionary; it does not supply a new holographic dual.
