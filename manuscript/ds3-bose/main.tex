\begin{abstract}
We construct the two-identical-particle sector of the previously specified
zero-gravity observer model by descending the matter permutation through
group averaging. Canonical Fock normalization gives a direct term plus
an exchange term, rather than a universal factor of two. The exchange
matrix element is the existing physical scalar Racah element within the
full channel space. The regulated two-creation order difference vanishes
exactly at coincident times. A spherical test proves that its quadratic
small-time coefficient is strictly positive for the compact real packet
considered here. Raw quadrature and precision failures are retained;
no finite-gravity or complete-Fock construction is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Model and labelled Hilbert space}
Use radius $\ell=1$, Newton constant $G_N=0$, proper $SO^+(3,1)$,
two distinguishable scalar BD observers with mass labels at least
$s>0$, and a single free KG-normalized scalar matter species $p>0$.
Couplings are one, $\rho(c)=2c^2/\pi$, and
$\hh(c)=\sqrt{1+c^2}$. Keep the same real nonnegative initial packet:
\[
 \rho(c)|f(c)|^2=\frac{630}{0.4}x^4(1-x)^4
 \mathbf1_{[0.4,0.8]}(c),\qquad x=(c-0.4)/0.4 .
\]
It has unit norm and its zero extension is $C^1$.
Numerical examples take $s=0.2$, $p=0.6$.
Every creation block carries the already defined half-period regulator
$e^{\pi H_{\rm out}/2}A e^{-\pi H_{\rm in}/2}$.
At real time $t$ the convention is
$A(t)=e^{-iH_{\rm out}t}A e^{iH_{\rm in}t}$.
These are creation blocks, not self-adjoint unsmeared fields.

First temporarily label the two equal-mass matter slots.
In the completed physical four-body space $\HH_{\rm lab}$ the slot
permutation $\FF$ obeys
\[
 \FF^\dagger=\FF,\qquad \FF^2=1,\qquad
 \PP=(1+\FF)/2 .
\]
Indeed, on finite-$K$, compact-mass tests it commutes with the diagonal
group action. The convergent four-body group average therefore satisfies
$\eta(\FF u,\FF v)=\eta(u,v)$ and
$\eta(u,\FF v)=\eta(\FF u,v)$.
It preserves the null space, descends to the quotient, and extends by
continuity as a unitary involution. The Bose space is $\PP\HH_{\rm lab}$.
This argument concerns the full four-body space, including all internal
spins, rather than the scalar subblock in a selected pairing.

\section{Fock normalization and the exchange form}
For orthonormal matter modes, let $M_j(t)$ be the observer operator
multiplying $a_j^\dagger$. On a finite-mode core a labelled chain has
coefficients
\[
 C_{jk}=M_j(t_2)M_k(t_1)f .
\]
Direct use of
$a_j^\dagger|n_j\rangle=\sqrt{n_j+1}|n_j+1\rangle$
gives the symmetric tensor
\begin{equation}
 B(t_2,t_1)=\sqrt2\,\PP C
           =\frac{C+\FF C}{\sqrt2}.
 \label{focknorm}
\end{equation}
The factor $\sqrt2$ is the usual Fock factor \cite{dg}.
It is not an additional choice of rigging-map or Haar normalization.
The group-average argument above and the bounded regulated labelled
$1\to2$ block extend \eqref{focknorm} to the physical completion. In particular
$\|A^+_{B,1\to2}\|\le\sqrt2\|A^+_{{\rm lab},1\to2}\|$.
This does not construct the whole Fock tower or a common self-adjoint
field domain.

For any two labelled chain states $k,l$,
\begin{equation}
 \langle B_k,B_l\rangle
 =\langle k,l\rangle+\langle k,\FF l\rangle,\qquad
 0\le\|B_k\|^2\le2\|k\|^2 .
 \label{boseform}
\end{equation}
The exchange expectation may have either sign for general $k$.
Equality to $2\|k\|^2$ requires $\FF k=k$.
Noncommuting observer coefficients do not ensure this.
Setting two distinguishable species labels equal only constructs
$\HH_{\rm lab}$; it does not apply the Bose projection.

Use the previous $0000=1$ scalar intertwiners $J_b$ in the pairing
$(a,p)|(c,p)$ and
\[
 W_b=\rho(b)K_{abp}K_{bcp},\qquad e_b=\sqrt{W_b}J_b .
\]
The flip maps $J_d$ to the opposite pairing, with the same spherical
normalization. Thus
\[
 U_{00}(b,d;a,p,c,p)=\langle e_b,\FF e_d\rangle .
\]
This is Hermitian as a kernel in $b,d$. Its full-space origin implies
contraction bounds; one must not require its scalar restriction to
square to one. The physical normalization and shadow phases are those
fixed in the earlier LPRS dictionary \cite{lprs}.
No further factorial or shadow-reflection factor is inserted.
The direct/crossed decomposition also has clear precedents in the
two-dimensional observer calculation \cite{kl}.

\section{Two times and a stable order difference}
Define the real base amplitude, supported at $a,b,c\ge s$,
\[
 T_b(a,c)=\frac{f(c)E_{abp}E_{bcp}}{\sqrt{W_b}}\,
       e^{\pi[\hh(a)-\hh(c)]/2},
 \qquad
 \alpha_b=e^{-it[\hh(a)-\hh(b)]},\quad
 \beta_b=e^{-it[\hh(b)-\hh(c)]}.
\]
Let $k=\int T_b\alpha_b e_b\,\dd b$ and
$l=\int T_b\beta_b e_b\,\dd b$, with the outer direct integrals in
$a,c$ understood. The physical Bose states are
$B_{t0}=\sqrt2\PP k$ and $B_{0t}=\sqrt2\PP l$.
Put
\[
 N=\langle k,k\rangle=\langle l,l\rangle,\quad
 Y=\langle k,l\rangle,\quad
 E_1=\langle k,\FF k\rangle,\quad E_2=\langle l,\FF l\rangle,\quad
 Z=\langle k,\FF l\rangle .
\]
Then
\begin{align}
 \|B_{t0}\|^2&=N+E_1,&
 \|B_{0t}\|^2&=N+E_2,\\
 \langle B_{t0},B_{0t}\rangle&=Y+Z,&
 D_B(t)&=2N+E_1+E_2-2\operatorname{Re}(Y+Z).
 \label{expanded}
\end{align}
All exchange terms use the same unsymmetrized, complex $U_{00}$.
For example their common weight is
\[
 \pi_0(c)\frac{g(a)}{g(c)}
 \sqrt{\widehat R_p(a,b)\widehat R_p(b,c)
       \widehat R_p(a,d)\widehat R_p(d,c)}\,U_{00}(b,d;a,p,c,p),
\]
and the phases for $E_1,E_2,Z$ are respectively
\[
 e^{it[\hh(d)-\hh(b)]},\quad e^{it[\hh(b)-\hh(d)]},\quad
 e^{it[\hh(a)+\hh(c)-\hh(b)-\hh(d)]}.
\]
The direct terms use the positive density
$\pi_0(c)g(a)\widehat R_p(a,b)\widehat R_p(b,c)/g(c)$.
Here
\[
 g(x)=\frac{x e^{\pi/(\sqrt{1+x^2}+x)}}{1+e^{-2\pi x}},\quad
 \widehat R_p(a,b)=
 \frac{A-B}{8\pi(A+\cosh\pi p)(B+\cosh\pi p)},
\]
with $A=\cosh\pi(a+b)$ and $B=\cosh\pi(a-b)$.

For computation set $e_b^{\rm en}=2\hh(b)-\hh(a)-\hh(c)$ and
\[
 \delta_b=\alpha_b-\beta_b
 =2i e^{-it[\hh(a)-\hh(c)]/2}
       \sin(te_b^{\rm en}/2).
\]
The stable formula is
\begin{equation}
 D_B=\|\delta T\|^2+\langle\delta T,\FF\,\delta T\rangle .
 \label{stable}
\end{equation}
It gives $D_B(0)=0$ algebraically, without subtracting large nearly
equal overlaps. Equal masses alone do not justify deleting the exchange
term. A four-\emph{field} vacuum correlator would also include the
intermediate vacuum contraction; it is not computed by this
two-creation sector.

\section{A strict small-time result}
All polynomial mass moments of $T$ are finite. For instance the compact
initial support, $g(a)\le a+C_0$, and
$\widehat R_p(a,b)\le e^{-\pi|a-b|}/(4\pi)$
give an exponentially integrable majorant in the two mass differences.
Consequently $j_b=e_b^{\rm en}T_b$ is in the labelled physical space.
Multiplication by the common outer phase in $\delta_b$ is unitary and
commutes with $\FF$. Using
$|\sin y-y|\le |y|^3/6$ gives
\begin{equation}
 D_B(t)=t^2 Q_B+O(t^4),\qquad
 Q_B=\|j\|^2+\langle j,\FF j\rangle
     =\|\sqrt2\PP j\|^2\ge0 .
 \label{short}
\end{equation}
This follows in Hilbert norm, with the sixth mass moment controlling
the remainder. Equation \eqref{stable} is even in real $t$.

We now show $Q_B>0$ for the stated packet.
The exact local vertex from the preceding note is
\[
 E_{abp}=C_p\sqrt{\frac{\cosh\pi b}{\cosh\pi a}}H_p(a,b),\quad
 C_p=\frac1{4\sqrt\pi}\sqrt{\frac{\sinh\pi p}{p}}.
\]
\[
 H_p(a,b)=r_p(a-b)-r_p(a+b),\qquad
 r_p(x)=\frac1{\cosh\pi x+\cosh\pi p}.
\]
The all-spherical component is invariant under the matter flip.
The derivative of the Bose order difference at zero has this component
equal to $i\sqrt2 f(c)$ times a positive common leg factor times
\[
 J(a,c)=\int_s^\infty H_p(a,b)H_p(b,c)
           [2\hh(b)-\hh(a)-\hh(c)]\,\dd b .
\]
For $a=c>s$ this becomes
$2\int_s^\infty H_p(c,b)^2[\hh(b)-\hh(c)]\,\dd b$.
Pair $b=c-x$ with $b=c+x$, for $0<x<c-s$. Since $r_p$ strictly
decreases on the positive axis,
\[
 H_p(c,c+x)>H_p(c,c-x)>0 .
\]
Strict convexity and monotonicity of $\hh$ also imply
\[
 \hh(c+x)-\hh(c)>\hh(c)-\hh(c-x)>0 .
\]
The paired contribution is therefore strictly positive, and the
remaining $b>2c-s$ contribution is positive. Thus $J(c,c)>0$.

Continuity, with exponential domination, implies positivity in some
uniform strip $|a-c|<\varepsilon$ for $c$ in any compact interval
$I\subset(0.4,0.8)$, with $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small to keep
$a>s$. Choose an all-spherical compact-mass test with coefficient
$f(c)\mathbf1_I(c)\mathbf1_{|a-c|<\varepsilon}$.
Its four-body group-average norm is finite, and its pairing with
$\sqrt2\PP j$ is strictly positive.
Physical Cauchy--Schwarz proves $Q_B>0$.
Hence $D_B(t)>0$ for sufficiently small nonzero $t$.
The proof establishes existence of the strip and time neighborhood;
no numerical size for either is certified. This effect is a finite-mass
observer result at $G_N=0$, not a gravitational chaos exponent.

\section{Mass tails and a free benchmark}
Let $T_L$ restrict $a,b\le L$ and retain the whole initial packet.
The preceding compact-packet bounds apply at $q=p$:
\[
 \|T-T_L\|^2\le\epsilon_L,\qquad \|T\|^2\le N_{\max}.
\]
Time phases do not change these norms.
The direct inner-product tail is at most $\epsilon_L$, since the
cutoff commutes with the phase multipliers.
The exchange tail is at most $2\sqrt{\epsilon_LN_{\max}}$.
For \eqref{stable}, the direct tail is at most $4\epsilon_L$ and the
exchange tail at most $8\sqrt{\epsilon_LN_{\max}}$. Thus
\begin{equation}
 |D_B-D_{B,L}|
 \le4\epsilon_L+8\sqrt{\epsilon_LN_{\max}} .
\end{equation}
This bound is not optimized for $t\to0$ and does not certify tiny
numerical differences at short times.
For $L=12$,
$\epsilon_L\le1.16353\,10^{-16}$,
$N_{\max}\le1.36456\,10^{-3}$, and the right side is
$3.18767\,10^{-9}$. The internal physical spectrum remains infinite.

In a fixed-observer free benchmark, let $u_t$ be a one-particle vector
with $\langle u_t,u_0\rangle=L_p(t)$, where
\[
 L_p(t)=\frac{\sin(pt)}{4\pi\sinh(\pi p)\sinh t},\qquad
 L_p(0)=\frac{p}{4\pi\sinh(\pi p)}.
\]
The Fock identity gives
\[
 \|a^\dagger(u_t)a^\dagger(u_0)\Omega\|^2
 =L_p(0)^2+|L_p(t)|^2,\qquad D_{\rm free}(t)=0.
\]
At $t=0$ the norm is $2L_p(0)^2$.
This is the required free target. We have not yet established convergence
of the complete Bose exchange term to it as observer mass tends to
infinity; the earlier noncrossed limit alone is insufficient.

\section{Numerics, independent checks, and limitations}

The occupation-basis tests independently construct finite Fock matrices
with three observer levels and four matter modes, including
noncommuting observer coefficients. A separate finite cyclic-group
average tests permutation descent algebraically; it is not a numerical
replacement for noncompact de Sitter group averaging.
High-precision one-dimensional integrals check the positive diagonal
pairing argument.

A sample initially intended as regular, $(a,b,c,d)=(0.7,0.5,0.6,0.8)$, lies
extremely close to $a+c=b+d$ at binary64 precision.
The old vectorized formula loses about $160\%$ relative accuracy there
and fails flip Hermiticity. The original failed diagnostic is retained.
Selected-point reevaluation at 55 and 75 decimal digits restores
consistency; it does not certify the whole vectorized grid.
Another retained far-tail point has roughly $0.128\%$ relative error.
No raw kernel is silently made real, symmetrized, or replaced.
The scientific proof of \eqref{short} does not depend on this
special-function numerical representation.

Next work must establish stable large-mass crossed limits and their
free Bose benchmark, then address the full Fock domain and finite
gravity. The present note does not complete de Sitter holography.
