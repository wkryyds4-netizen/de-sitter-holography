\begin{abstract}
We continue reproducing a specified bounded Kim--Scarinci pentagon
representation. Additional literature does not yet supply a directly
matched kernel. A standard residual estimate reduces verification of
saved finite Hermite chains to seven two-dimensional overlaps.
Two chirp overlaps are evaluated by Gaussian moments in ball arithmetic.
All five quantum-dilogarithm overlaps are certified using an existing
Gauss--Legendre error theorem and Mehler bounds. Their combination gives
$\|Df\|>0.04489$ for the precisely defined $\hbar=1$ continuous word
and normalized Gaussian below. This is a computer-assisted statement
about that literal spectral representation, not a finite-$G$ observer
correlator or a conclusion about the viability of de Sitter holography.
\end{abstract}

\section{What the new literature does and does not supply}
Belousov--Sarkissian--Spiridonov \cite{bss}, equations (2.5),
(4.2)--(4.5), (4.18), (5.9)--(5.11), and (6.16), retain an integer
angular label. The binomial formula is a sum over that label and an
integral over a continuous variable; its degeneration is taken as
$b\to i$. Equation (4.12) requires real angular Fourier parameter and
$\operatorname{Im}h>-1$. These are useful explicit formulae, but do not
identify the fixed-deformation additive $\R^2$ phase below.
In particular, the two real dimensions of a complex integration
variable do not remove the discrete label of its Mellin--Fourier
transform.

Andersen--Kashaev \cite{ak}, equations (103), (117)--(122) and
Lemma 6, use $A_N=\R\oplus\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$ with measure
$N^{-1/2}\sum_n dx$, positive integer $N$, and $|\theta|=1$,
$\operatorname{Re}\theta>0$. Their Fourier formula and finite Weyl
operators require that cyclic spectral component. An explicit
intertwining identification with the present representation is still
missing. This is an applicability check, not a claim that no such
identification can exist.

For validated integration we use existing Arb/Petras algorithms
\cite{johansson,flint}, rather than inventing a new quadrature theorem.
The caller must supply valid complex enclosures and check analyticity.
Our calculation below does not claim a new general mathematical result.

\section{Residuals of actual saved vectors}
Keep $\hbar=1$, $P_j=-\pi i\partial_j$, $Q=(X,Y)$,
$P=(P_y,P_x)$, and the phase $F=F_1$ defined in KS \cite{ks}.
The operators
\[
 A=F(Q),\qquad B=F(P),\qquad M=e^{iXY/\pi}
\]
are unitary on $L^2(\R^2,dx\,dy/(2\pi^2))$. The tested word is
\[
 D=AB-BM^{-1}BMA .
\]
No heat multiplier is inserted in this section.

Let $v_0=f$ and let $v_j$ be any chosen finite vectors approximating
$U_jv_{j-1}$. Define
\[
 e_j=\|U_jv_{j-1}-v_j\|.
\]
Unitarity and the triangle inequality, iterated one step at a time, give
\[
 \|U_m\cdots U_1f-v_m\|\le\sum_{j=1}^m e_j .
\]
For saved left and right chains $l_0=r_0=f$ this yields
\begin{equation}
 \|Df\|\ge \|l_2-r_5\|-\sum_{j=1}^2e_{L,j}
                           -\sum_{j=1}^5e_{R,j}.                 \label{bound}
\end{equation}
Every step error is determined by one overlap:
\begin{equation}
 e_j^2=\|v_{j-1}\|^2+\|v_j\|^2
            -2\operatorname{Re}\langle v_j,U_jv_{j-1}\rangle .
                                                               \label{step}
\end{equation}
These statements do not require the saved vectors to be exact
orthogonal projections. Consequently they avoid interpreting a
quadrature-dependent norm loss as an already certified leakage.

The left steps are $(B,A)$; the right steps are $(A,M,B,M^{-1},B)$.
For a $B$ step, Fourier transform both finite vectors. The Hermite
Fourier factors are exactly $(-i)^{m+n}$, so the remaining overlap is
again a coordinate integral, with multiplier $F(y,x)$. At $\hbar=1$
the exact phase is symmetric. Thus five $F$ overlaps and two chirp
overlaps suffice, without nested Fourier kernels or an assumption
that a single chiral factor acts on the Gaussian.

All saved complex binary64 coefficients are lifted as exact dyadic
numbers. They specify legitimate finite vectors independently of how
accurately they approximated a projection. The initial state is the
exact normalized oscillator vacuum, not a subsequently rounded
normalization.

\section{Gaussian moments certify the chirp steps}
In oscillator coordinates $z=x/\sqrt\pi$, $t=y/\sqrt\pi$, a normalized
Hermite expansion has the form
\[
 v(z,t)=\pi^{-1/2}e^{-(z^2+t^2)/2}
        \sum_{m,n}c_{mn}p_m(z)p_n(t),\qquad
 p_m(z)=\frac{H_m(z)}{\sqrt{2^m m!}} .
\]
The coefficient norm is its Hilbert norm. Multiplication by $M^{s}$,
$s=\pm1$, inserts $e^{istz}$. For polynomials $V,W$ specified by
these coefficients,
\[
 \langle w,M^sv\rangle
 =\frac1\pi\int_{\R^2}e^{-z^2-t^2+istz}\,
                    \overline{W(z,t)}V(z,t)\,dz\,dt .
\]
Conjugation here acts on polynomial coefficients on the real plane.
The Gaussian prefactor is $2/\sqrt5$ and the normalized covariance is
\[
 C_s=\begin{pmatrix}2/5&is/5\\is/5&2/5\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Writing its normalized moments as $\mu_{mn}$, one has
\[
 \mu_{00}=1,\quad \mu_{0,2k}=(2k-1)!!(2/5)^k,\quad
 \mu_{0,2k+1}=0,
\]
\[
 \mu_{mn}=(m-1)\frac25\mu_{m-2,n}
                  +n\frac{is}{5}\mu_{m-1,n-1}\quad(m\ge1).
\]
A finite polynomial product therefore gives the overlap without
quadrature, boundary truncation or spectral-tail approximation.
Ball arithmetic bounds rounding and cancellation in this evaluation.

\section{A quantitative conditional target}
Suppose, for each of the five full $F$ overlaps,
\[
 \operatorname{Re}\langle v_j,U_jv_{j-1}\rangle
                           \ge \|v_j\|^2-\eta .
\]
Then (\ref{step}) gives
$e_j\le\sqrt{\|v_{j-1}\|^2-\|v_j\|^2+2\eta}$
for these particular saved vectors. For the size-128 chain,
(\ref{bound}) would give respectively
$0.05208026006$ at $\eta=10^{-5}$ and $0.02048259469$ at
$\eta=10^{-4}$. These were initially conditional targets. The completed
calculation below proves all five hypotheses for $\eta=10^{-4}$, but
not for $\eta=10^{-5}$, and gives a stronger bound using their actual
individual error enclosures.

\section{A validated local pilot and the full-plane task}
For $\hbar=1$ use the finite product
\[
 F_N(x,y)=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}
 \frac{1+e^{x-(2j+1)\pi-iy}}{1+e^{x-(2j+1)\pi+iy}}
 \frac{1+e^{y-(2j+1)\pi-ix}}{1+e^{y-(2j+1)\pi+ix}} .
\]
For real $|x|,|y|\le L_x$ with
$r=e^{L_x-(2N+1)\pi}<1$, its exact-arithmetic tail obeys
\[
 |F-F_N|\le\frac{4r}{(1-e^{-2\pi})(1-r)} .
\]
On a rectangle avoiding the product singularities, the remaining
finite integrand is meromorphic in its complexified variables.
In a nested integral with real inner coordinate $z$, poles require
$\operatorname{Re}t$ to lie on an odd multiple of $\sqrt\pi$.
The outer callback therefore rejects any complex ball intersecting
one of these rows before asserting analyticity. It never applies
ordinary conjugation or absolute value to a complex integration
variable.

A pilot for the size-64 first right step on $(z,t)\in[-1,1]^2$,
$N=8$, encloses the finite-product overlap near
\[
 0.710145049-0.0000006487\,i .
\]
The stored enclosure has sub-$10^{-9}$ width; the product tail is
separately bounded. This is one compact square, not the full overlap.

For the size-128 first step, the input is exactly Gaussian.
Outside $[-L,L]^2$, Cauchy--Schwarz bounds the omitted overlap by
\[
 \sqrt{2\operatorname{erfc}(L)}\,\|w\| .
\]
Removing horizontal strips of half-width $\rho$ around $K$ pole rows
costs at most $2K\rho\,\mathcal K_n\|w\|$, where the standard Mehler
formula gives, for $0<r_0<1$,
\[
 \mathcal K_n=\frac{r_0^{-(n-1)}}{\sqrt{\pi(1-r_0^2)}} .
\]
This follows by bounding the one-coordinate Hermite projection kernel
and applying Cauchy--Schwarz in the other coordinate. Together with
the product tail it gives an explicit omission budget. This nested
approach was discontinued; the tensor method below certifies the first
full overlap. Remaining steps need their own tail bounds and complete
overlap enclosures.

\section{A uniform outer bound for nested integration}
There is a computational distinction between evaluating the inner
integral at a nearly exact outer node and bounding it on an entire
complex ball. Asking the inner integrator to resolve parameter
uncertainty to a tiny tolerance can waste subdivisions. For the
Gaussian-input step, Mehler's formula \cite{mehler} supplies the
following uniform bound instead.

Let $I_N(t)$ be the inner $z$ integral of the finite-product Gaussian
overlap, over any finite real interval. Set $u=\sqrt\pi\operatorname{Re}t$,
$v=\sqrt\pi\operatorname{Im}t$, $a_j=(2j+1)\pi$, and
\[
 G_N(u,v)=\frac{e^{2N|v|}}{|\cos(u/2)|^N}
       \prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{1+e^{u-a_j}}{|1-e^{u-a_j}|}.
\]
This is finite away from the excluded pole rows. For real $x$, the
first product ratio is bounded by $e^{2|v|}/|\cos(u/2)|$, since
$|1+Re^{iu}|\ge(1+R)|\cos(u/2)|$ for $R\ge0$.
The second ratio is at most $(1+e^{u-a_j})/|1-e^{u-a_j}|$ by the
triangle inequalities. These estimates retain all finite factors.

For any $0<r<1$, the finite Hermite polynomial kernel satisfies
\[
 \sum_{k=0}^{n-1}|p_k(z)|^2
 \le\frac{r^{-(n-1)}}{\sqrt{1-r^2}}
 \exp\left(\frac{2r(\operatorname{Re}z)^2}{1+r}
           +\frac{2r(\operatorname{Im}z)^2}{1-r}\right).
\]
Apply this estimate to both coordinates, use coefficient
Cauchy--Schwarz for $W$, multiply by the vacuum Gaussian, and extend
the absolute inner Gaussian integral to the real line. The result is
\[
 |I_N(t)|\le
 \frac{\|w\|r^{-(n-1)}}{\sqrt{\pi(1-r)}}
 \exp\left(-\frac{(\operatorname{Re}t)^2}{1+r}
              +\frac{(\operatorname{Im}t)^2}{1-r}\right)
 G_N(u,v).
\]
Each of several chosen $r$ values gives a valid bound, so the smallest
upper endpoint may be used. The outer callback returns a containing
complex ball from this inequality for its analytic-bound requests;
nearly exact quadrature nodes still use actual inner integration.
Holomorphy is checked by the pole-row guard, not inferred from a
finite numerical answer. This nested run was subsequently stopped
without a completed segment; the direct tensor method below replaces it.

\section{Direct use of an existing quadrature theorem}
Johansson's Section 2 \cite{johansson} states that an analytic function
bounded by $M$ on a Bernstein ellipse of parameter $\rho>1.1$ has
$n_q$-point Gauss--Legendre error at most $50M\rho^{-2n_q}$.
We use this existing result with $\rho=2$, whose semiaxes are $5/4,3/4$;
we do not claim a new quadrature theorem.

For a rectangle with half-widths $h_x,h_y$, let $M_x$ bound the
integrand on the $x$ ellipse times the real $y$ interval, and let $M_y$
bound it on the real $x$ interval times the $y$ ellipse. Positivity
and sum $2$ of the reference weights, together with
\[
 I_xI_y-Q_xQ_y=(I_x-Q_x)I_y+Q_x(I_y-Q_y),
\]
give the tensor error bound
\[
 E_R=100h_xh_y\,2^{-2n_q}(M_x+M_y).
\]
The scaled integrand is the analytic extension of
$\overline{w(z,t)}F_N(\sqrt\pi z,\sqrt\pi t)h_0(z)h_0(t)$:
only coefficients are conjugated in its extension.
Every product denominator must exclude zero on the bounding complex
rectangle. Otherwise no analytic quadrature bound is accepted.

For a saved $n\times n$ vector, Mehler's formula and coefficient
Cauchy--Schwarz give the following amplitude bound for any $0<r<1$:
\[
 |\overline{w}_{\rm an}(z,t)h_0(z)h_0(t)|
 \le \frac{\|w\|r^{-(n-1)}}{\pi\sqrt{1-r^2}}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{(\Re z)^2+(\Re t)^2}{1+r}
             +\frac{(\Im z)^2+(\Im t)^2}{1-r}\right].
\]
The code chooses among eight valid values $r=1-2^{-k}$.
On real cells $|F_N|=1$ almost everywhere. A cell touching a singularity
can therefore contribute zero to the numerical sum while its full
absolute integral bound is added to the error. No region is silently
deleted. Dyadic subdivision covers the whole square exactly.

For $[-L,L]^2$, the remaining errors are bounded by
\[
 T\le\|w\|\sqrt{2\operatorname{erfc}(L)},\qquad
 P\le\frac{4r_N\|w\|}{(1-e^{-2\pi})(1-r_N)},\quad
 r_N=e^{L\sqrt\pi-(2N+1)\pi}<1.
\]
The first bound uses the exact vacuum input and Cauchy--Schwarz;
it must not be transferred to a general finite Hermite input.

\section{General finite pairs: weighted Mehler bounds}
For a finite coefficient array $c$ define, for $0<r<1$,
\[
 A_c(r)=\sum_{m,n}|c_{mn}|^2r^{-m-n},\quad
 X_c(r)=\sum_{m,n}|c_{mn}|^2r^{-m},\quad
 Y_c(r)=\sum_{m,n}|c_{mn}|^2r^{-n},\quad a_r=\frac{1-r}{1+r}.
\]
Weighted Cauchy--Schwarz and the standard Mehler formula give
\[
 |\overline{w}_{\rm an}(z,t)v(z,t)|\le
 \frac{\sqrt{A_v(r)A_w(r)}}{\pi(1-r^2)}
 \exp\left[-a_r\big((\Re z)^2+(\Re t)^2\big)
       +a_r^{-1}\big((\Im z)^2+(\Im t)^2\big)\right].
\]
For the exterior of a real square, integrate the other coordinate
first using Hermite orthogonality, and apply the one-coordinate
Mehler bound. The union bound gives
\[
 T_c(L):=\|1_{\R^2\setminus[-L,L]^2}c\|^2
 \le\frac{X_c(r)+Y_c(r)}{1-r}\operatorname{erfc}(\sqrt{a_r}L).
\]
Each chosen $r$ is a valid bound. Minimize the upper endpoints
separately for $v,w$; the omitted overlap is at most
$\sqrt{T_v(L)T_w(L)}$. This handles the entire finite vectors,
including every retained high Hermite mode, without treating them
as Gaussian inputs. It is an application of existing identities,
not a new general theorem.

\paragraph{A sharper phase enclosure.}
For real $u,b,y$ write $x=u+ib$, $a_j=(2j+1)\pi$, and
\[
 d(s,\theta)=|1+e^{s+i\theta}|e^{-s}
 =\sqrt{(e^{-s}-1)^2+4e^{-s}\cos^2(\theta/2)}.
\]
Subtract the conjugate denominator from the numerator of each
factor. The triangle inequality then yields
\[
 |F_N(u+ib,y)|\le\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}
 \left(1+\frac{2|\sin b|}{d(u-a_j,b+y)}\right)
 \left(1+\frac{|e^{2b}-1|}{d(y-a_j-b,u)}\right).
\]
For a $y$ ellipse interchange coordinates, using the symmetry of
$F_N$ at $\hbar=1$. All denominator lower bounds must be strictly
positive. Zero-containing bounds reject analytic quadrature and
trigger subdivision or a real absolute-integral bound.
The initial implementation used interval powers across zero, which
can return NaN in this interface. The production version squares
nonnegative lower endpoints, preserving rigorous denominator bounds.

\section{The precise continuous representation being tested}
The state in the original measure is
\[
 f(x,y)=\sqrt{2}\,e^{-(x^2+y^2)/(2\pi)},\qquad
 \int_{\R^2}|f|^2\frac{dx\,dy}{2\pi^2}=1.
\]
The unitary coordinate change to the oscillator convention sends
this state to $h_0(z)h_0(t)$, with $z=x/\sqrt\pi,t=y/\sqrt\pi$.
The infinite product is the almost-everywhere limit of the displayed
$F_N$. Values on its discrete singular set can be assigned arbitrarily
with modulus one without changing the multiplication operator.

The source conventions were checked again against KS Section 6.3,
equation (35), and Proposition 6.15, and against the pure-imaginary
parameter formulas in Kim's survey, Section 3 \cite{trilogy}.
With $q=e^{-\pi}$ the two compact-product ratios give precisely the
displayed symmetric $F(x,y)$ at $\hbar=1$; this check does not use a
formal cancellation of unbounded operators.

For $P_x=-\pi i\partial_x$, the exact translation action gives
\[
 e^{isX}e^{itP_x}=e^{-i\pi st}e^{itP_x}e^{isX}.
\]
The analogous formula holds for $(Y,P_y)$; the other coordinate and
momentum pairs commute strongly. Consequently the ordered quadruple
$(X,Y,P_y,P_x)$ obeys the Weyl assumptions stated in Proposition 6.15.
Moreover, by direct differentiation and unitary conjugation,
\[
 M^{-1}P_yM=P_y+X,\qquad M^{-1}P_xM=P_x+Y.
\]
The two sums strongly commute since they are simultaneous unitary
conjugates of $P_y,P_x$. Bounded joint spectral calculus therefore
identifies $M^{-1}BM$ with $F(X+P_y,Y+P_x)$.

These identities specify the literal continuous test independently
of any finite matrix commutators. The finite vectors are only
auxiliary comparison vectors in the residual inequality.
If their certified residual sum is below their final separation,
the conclusion concerns this exact bounded word on $L^2(\R^2)$.
Its comparison with the published assertion must retain the explicit
definitions above and the distinction between a literal spectral
operator and a proposed analytic continuation prescription.

No observable time, observer mass, gravitational coupling $G$, or
physical four-point gluing measure has been assigned to these
operators. A nonzero word would obstruct using this literal
implementation as an already verified physical building block; it
would not rule out de Sitter holography or every other representation.
