           A query concerning the positive-curvature pentagon
                   and its analytic-continuation proof
                        Technical note for discussion with the authors

                                          6 November 2026


                                               Abstract
          This note concerns the ordinary spectral-calculus interpretation of the operators in Kim–
      Scarinci, arXiv:2112.13329v2 [KS]. We identify a false cross strong-commutation assertion in
      the proof of Proposition 6.11, a moving-pole obstruction to the stated analytic-continuation
      argument, and an apparent missing factor of i in (75). A fresh interval recomputation of a
      public certificate supports a counterexample to the Λ = 1 part of Proposition 6.15, including
      equality up to a scalar phase. We give a conservative replacement of that proposition
      for Λ = −1, 0, with proofs. We have not constructed a replacement positive-curvature
      quantization. We would welcome correction of any mismatch with the authors’ intended
      operator realization.

3    The cross strong-commutation assertion is false
For τ > 0, let
                                  N = X − iτ Y,             T = Py + iτ Px .
Both are normal operators, defined by the joint spectral calculus of their respective commuting
self-adjoint components. On the Schwartz core,

                                    [N, T ] = 0,         [N, T ∗ ] = 2πτ I.

The first equality is only formal commutation; it is not spectral strong commutation. There is a
direct bounded-operator obstruction avoiding unbounded-product domain questions. If N and
T strongly commuted, all their bounded Borel functions would commute. In particular, since

Re N = X and Im T /τ = Px , the unitaries eiX and eiPx would commute. Equation (1) instead
gives
                      eiX eiPx = −eiPx eiX ,   ∥[eiX , eiPx ]u∥ = 2∥u∥.
Thus the cross strong-commutation assertion immediately before (74) is false in this representation.
The other assertion in that sentence, strong commutation of X − iτ Y with X + iτ Y , is valid.
    More generally, for h = a + ib, the formally commuting pair Nh = X − hY , Th = Py + hPx
has [Nh , Th∗ ] = 2πbI on the Schwartz core. This explains the loss of spectral commutation when
moving from real to nonreal parameters. It invalidates the stated reason for moving the chiral
factor; it does not, by itself, exclude an exceptional identity for some particular functions on a
specially chosen domain. Such an identity would need a different proof. Section 6 concerns the
separate question of the final bounded pentagon.


4     An apparent coefficient typo in (75)
Writing U = X + hY and V = Py + hPx gives [U, V ] = 2πih formally. For real h > 0, the known
difference relation for Φh , with the usual domain justification for the real-parameter intertwiner,
yields

                                Φh (U )eV = eV Φh (U + 2πih)
                                          = eV (1 + eπih eU )Φh (U ).

Accordingly the factor eπh in (75) should be eπih . The original TeX contains the former, so this
is not an HTML conversion issue. At h = iτ , the corrected factor becomes e−πτ , consistently
with (74). This local correction does not repair either strong commutation or the continuation
argument. The residual calculation uses (2), not the coefficient in (75).

6.2    Independence and a reproducibility caveat
The interval integrator and chirp-moment implementation were reused from the repository after
inspection; the interval run is a fresh recomputation, not a wholly independent implementation or
a proof-assistant verification. The partition and coefficient data were also reused. The finite-vector
assembly, FFT diagnostic, scalar Barnes-integral comparison and binary64 cell cross-check were
written separately. All fresh analytic-cell integral enclosures overlapped the saved enclosures.
Independent 32-point quadrature sums differed from the corresponding saved 24-point sums by
at most approximately 3.4 × 10−16 ; this comparison itself is not certified.
    The original replay audit did not pass unchanged on this machine: 9,890 checks failed when
comparing freshly computed error-bound upper endpoints with saved singleton upper endpoints
(audit_tensor_chain.py, line 74). No other failure category was reported. The largest change
in a summed local-bound budget was about 2.32 × 10−14 ; no cell classification changed. Two
valid upper bounds need not have identical endpoints, but this does not excuse silently accepting
an audit failure. Accordingly, all those bounds were recomputed and the final certificate was
rebuilt using the new bounds. The precise cause of the endpoint discrepancy was not isolated.
This distinction between exact regression reproducibility and validity of a freshly enclosed bound
should remain explicit.
    The recomputation used Python 3.12.7, NumPy 1.26.4 and python-flint 0.9.0 on macOS ARM,
256-bit cell arithmetic, 1024-bit chirp arithmetic and 512-bit final assembly. The implementation
and its mathematical error estimates remain open to independent review.

7     A conservative replacement with proofs
The following corrects the scope of the pentagon proposition; it does not claim to reprove every
other assertion of [KS].

operators on a separable Hilbert space satisfying the full regular Weyl relations corresponding to

                 [x, x′ ] = [y, y ′ ] = [x, y] = [x′ , y ′ ] = 0,      [x, y ′ ] = [y, x′ ] = πiI.

Proof for Λ = −1. Use the established real-parameter Faddeev pentagon in the normalization of

                                 Φτ (u)Φτ (v) = Φτ (v)Φτ (u + v)Φτ (u).                                      (7)

complex-parameter continuation. Set

                    u = x + τ y,       v = x′ + τ y ′ ,       a = x − τ y,    b = x′ − τ y ′ .

pair strongly commutes with every member of the (a, b) pair: their symplectic pairings vanish, so
their Weyl unitaries commute. Apply (7) to (u, v) and also to (b, a); invert the latter to obtain

                          Φτ (a)−1 Φτ (b)−1 = Φτ (b)−1 Φτ (a + b)−1 Φτ (a)−1 .

8     Updated assessment and proposed correction
The present evidence goes beyond an unverified numerical discrepancy. The cross
strong-commutation assertion is analytically false in the stated regular Weyl realization, and the
continuation proof cannot stand as written. Separately, the checked correspondence in Section
2 and the fresh interval bounds in Section 6 give a computer-assisted counterexample to the
Λ = 1 part of Proposition 6.15 at ℏ = 1, under the ordinary spectral-calculus interpretation.
This latter conclusion depends on the validity of the interval estimates and their implementation;
it is not a wholly analytic or formally verified counterexample. If that certificate is valid, a
different proof cannot restore the same universally quantified statement: its scope or its operator
construction must change. A counterexample at one parameter suffices for this conclusion; it
does not establish failure at every positive parameter.
    Section 7 proves the restricted pentagon for Λ = −1, 0, using the known real-parameter Fad-
deev theorem in the negative-curvature case. It does not repair the Λ = 1 branch. Theorem 6.14
and other downstream claims relying on the disputed continuation require separate reassessment;
we have not refuted each of them individually.
    A conservative correction would make the following distinctions, while inviting the authors
to identify any error in our matching or certificate:

    1. Correct the coefficient in (75), and remove the cross strong-commutation assertion preceding
       (74).

    2. Restrict Proposition 6.15, with its present scalar functions and ordinary spectral-calculus
       meaning, to Λ = −1, 0. For Λ = 1, the computation above is evidence of failure of the
       stated identity, not only an omitted proof detail.

    3. Mark the positive-curvature proofs of Proposition 6.11 and Theorem 6.14, and claims
       explicitly relying on that continuation, as requiring new justification. This note does not
       independently refute every one of those statements or every constrained surface realization.

A possible corrigendum paragraph, offered as a draft rather than as an attributed author
statement, is:

     The positive-curvature analytic-continuation argument in Section 6 requires correction. Formal
     commutation of the chiral operators does not imply the cross strong commutation used before
     (74). Moreover, the fixed-vector holomorphy assertion following (75) is not justified for the
     stated spectral realization, which encounters moving poles. The coefficient in (75) should be
     eπih . Proposition 6.15 is retained here for Λ = −1, 0; its Λ = 1 statement for the specified
     paired spectral function is withdrawn, pending resolution of the reported counterexample.
     Positive-curvature conclusions using this proof require separate analysis.

Adding cross strong commutation as a hypothesis would contradict the nontrivial Weyl relations
in this representation. Restricting the final bounded identity to a dense domain would not help,
since it would extend to all of H by continuity. Changes at measure-zero spectral points, unitary
changes of polarization, and a constant phase likewise do not address this example. A different
spectral space, scalar function, or contour prescription might lead to a different valid theorem;
no such positive-curvature repair is proved here.
    We would particularly appreciate clarification of the following:

  1. Is ordinary joint spectral calculus the intended meaning of the positive-curvature factors
     and of Proposition 6.15, or is an additional contour or regularization prescription intended?

  2. Is there a specific additional hypothesis in Proposition 6.15 that excludes the regular Weyl
     representation and Gaussian in Section 2?

  3. If a different continuation is intended, what is its domain, and why does its endpoint equal
     the bounded paired multiplier F1τ used in the proposition?

  4. Can the explicit certificate and phase-independent bound be recomputed independently, or
     can an error in their normalization, ordering, error estimates, or representation matching
     be identified?
